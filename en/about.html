About the project

Vulnerable communities fostering innovation in urban food systems

FEED4FOOD is a European research and innovation project showing, on the ground, how urban and peri-urban agriculture can bring healthy food and new opportunities to a city's most exposed residents.

The challenge

In European cities, people on low incomes and vulnerable groups have the hardest time accessing healthy food. They are also the least involved in decisions about the local food system.

At the same time, cities are spreading over the farmland around them, and climate change is putting pressure on water resources and on local vegetable production.

Our mission

To empower vulnerable communities in European cities in Greece, Cyprus, Romania and Poland through urban agriculture living labs, and to promote the transition towards sustainable, inclusive and healthy low-impact urban food systems.

Objectives

What we set out to do

01

Pilot living labs

In 4 European cities, each focused on one or more vulnerable groups, showing how sustainable urban agriculture can include and empower them.

02

On-the-ground demonstrations

Together with authorities, communities and local actors: sustainable agriculture, waste valorisation, short supply chains and primary processing.

03

Evidence for decision-makers

We identify what holds back and what speeds up the uptake of these practices, as inspiration for policy-makers, communities, innovators and entrepreneurs.

04

Shared data and indicators

Scientific, social and technical indicators collected in each lab and integrated into a shared knowledge hub.

Approach

The three pillars of FEED4FOOD

Living labs

Spaces where we build social vegetable gardens, urban farms and community gardens, or, as in Bucharest, support existing peri-urban farms.

Central knowledge and learning hub

All data from the labs is analysed as an integrated system. The project is developing a live dashboard showing how each lab is performing.

Transition strategy

Co-learning and mutual mentoring between lab participants, so that the labs continue after the project ends.
